# Small-business education: demand validation before curriculum

Use for an exploratory request to research and plan education for small-business owners. This is a reusable planning pattern, not a claim that any particular course has proven demand.

## Evidence ladder

| Evidence | Supports | Does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Official business survey | Operating problems in its population and reference period | Training preference, local demand, AI suitability |
| Public course catalogue | Existing supply, topics and listed delivery terms | Course quality, effectiveness, paid demand |
| Owner interview about a recent task | Concrete task, frequency, current workaround | Population prevalence or willingness to pay without asking |
| Attendance/payment commitment | Demand under the stated offer and conditions | Sustained use or business impact |
| Repeated field use with review | Practical usability and observed workload | Causal revenue/ROI gains without a suitable design |

Attach sources beside factual claims and keep recommendations explicitly labeled. Separate survey year, release date and retrieval date. Reposted official material is not independent corroboration. Do not rank courses using views or stars without accounting for age, placement and exposure.

## Research starting points

- Ministry of SMEs and Startups, official survey release: https://www.mss.go.kr/site/smba/ex/bbs/View.do?cbIdx=86&bcIdx=1066231 — release dated 2026-03-13, referring to 2024. Historical example; retrieve the relevant current release before reusing any figures.
- SEMAS Knowledge Learning Centre: https://edu.sbiz.or.kr/ — inspect current public course listings. A catalogue page does not imply that individual lessons have been reviewed.

The research example distinguished competitive pressure and cost concerns from actual education preference, and digital/smart adoption from AI adoption. Those distinctions matter more than preserving snapshot percentages.

## Translate problems without forcing AI

- Customer acquisition → real differentiators, product descriptions, channel selection; AI drafts, owner verifies claims.
- Repeated inquiries → facts-based FAQ, response templates, escalation rules; no automatic dispatch.
- Margin uncertainty → bookkeeping categories and verified spreadsheet calculations; AI explains, qualified professionals handle advice where required.
- Repeated operations → opening/closing/handover checklists; responsible owner checks against real procedures and safety rules.
- Low digital fluency → accounts, files, privacy and source checking before advanced AI exercises.
- Tax/labor/legal concerns → preparation for professional consultation, not unqualified AI decisions.

## Interview guide

Ask about actual behavior before hypothetical interest:

1. What was the last occurrence of the task that consumed the most time?
2. How often does it occur, how long does it take, and who handles it?
3. What is the current workaround and where does it fail?
4. Which one problem would the owner improve first, and why?
5. What previous training was or was not applied? What prevented use?
6. Can the owner independently log in, save a file, and use the available device?
7. What completed artifact would make the session worthwhile?
8. What duration and time slots are feasible?
9. Under what conditions would they pay rather than take a free course? Ask price expectations without inventing market rates.
10. Will they repeatedly use the output and record review-inclusive time and corrections?

For institutional buyers add budget range, purchasing procedure, recruitable audience, equipment/support and reporting requirements. Never present proposed interviews as conducted. External invitations and collection of identifiable data remain subject to approval and privacy requirements.

## Narrow pilot pattern

Same recurring task → one small artifact → human review → two-week application → day-30 stop/revise/expand decision.

An introductory two-hour FAQ workshop may use a fictional shop fact sheet and five questions. Deliberately omit one fact: a successful participant identifies the omission and escalates instead of inventing an answer. Keep complaint compensation, refunds, health/safety assurances and automated publishing outside the starter scope.

Recruit based on task frequency and verification ability, even within one industry. A homogeneous industry is not proof of homogeneous need. If inquiry volume is low, use a product description or operating checklist instead. Advanced marketing, operations and financial-literacy modules are separate options, not required additions to the first two hours.

## Measurement and release gates

- Compute agenda totals and active-participation minutes with code.
- Define completeness/accuracy criteria for the actual artifact.
- Count drafting plus review and correction time, not generation time alone.
- Treat different-question classroom comparisons as measurement practice, not controlled evidence.
- Predefine safety stops; investigate serious policy errors or data exposure immediately.
- Label target completion counts as pilot design thresholds, never observed results.
- Do not attribute sales changes solely to education; consider seasonality, promotions and other changes.
- Distinguish curriculum plan from produced teaching assets: listing a backup recording or worksheet in a plan does not mean it exists.
- A useful commercial hypothesis is industry-specific output review plus follow-up, but validate willingness to pay before setting price or asserting differentiation succeeds.
