# Worked Example: Existing Founder Applying to a New-Business Program

## Pattern

A national startup-support notice allowed both pre-founders and founders within seven years, but existing founders had to pursue a different business. The governing test was not a broad industry label; it was whether the current and proposed activities differed at the five-digit KSIC level and whether the new entity qualified as a startup under the relevant law.

## Durable Lessons

1. Read the full official attachment, not only the press release. The attachment may specify different obligations by round.
2. Pull the applicant's actual registered activities from approved internal records before suggesting items.
3. Map each proposed item to a plausible five-digit KSIC code and compare it with every current business code.
4. Do not treat a code difference as final approval. Record the exact ambiguity and ask the regional implementing body to confirm it.
5. Check whether a new registration or new entity is required and at which round; adding a line of business to an existing registration may be insufficient.
6. Check same-year startup-commercialization exclusions before investing in the application.
7. Use regional selection ratios only as a favorable structural factor, never as a probability guarantee.
8. In crowded AI programs, search by functionality, buyer, and workflow—not just the proposed product name. If a direct competitor already combines translation, training, assessment, signatures, and records, narrow the job-to-be-done further.
9. Convert the research into three gates: legal eligibility, customer evidence, and differentiation. A conditional `GO` should name what evidence closes each gate.

## Example Candidate Screen

A useful scorecard for a small AI firm is:

| Criterion | Weight | Question |
|---|---:|---|
| Eligibility/classification distance | 25 | Can the item be a genuinely distinct, legally recognizable business? |
| Customer pain | 25 | Is the problem frequent, costly, and owned by a named buyer? |
| Differentiation | 20 | What exact workflow or proof does the incumbent not provide? |
| MVP feasibility | 15 | Can a credible prototype and pilot be completed inside the round? |
| Founder fit | 15 | Does the founder have capability, access, and evidence—not just interest? |

## Application-Evidence Pattern

A concise, credible pre-submission package contains:

- Two interviews with named buyer roles.
- One anonymized source document or workflow.
- A narrow five-screen prototype.
- One measurable baseline and target.
- A competitor table that explicitly states overlap.
- A 30–60 second end-to-end demonstration if optional media is accepted.

## Reporting Pattern

Lead with the deadline and the strongest disqualifier. Then state the recommended item in one sentence, give the eligibility judgment separately from program fit, and finish with reverse-dated actions. If policy requires posting the full report to an internal reporting channel, leave only a short completion notice in the originating thread and retain the delivery receipt/message identifier.
